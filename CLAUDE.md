# Apple Home Key → Yale nexTouch, on ESP32-C6

Tap an iPhone (Apple Home Key, Express Mode) on a reader hidden in a doorbell; the
system authenticates the key **offline** and unlocks a **Yale nexTouch** mortise lock
over **Bluetooth**, using the lock's `yalexs-ble` offline key. No cloud, no Home
Assistant and no Yale app in the unlock path. Home Assistant only feeds optional
extras (Yale cloud state, battery %) and consumes MQTT.

Fork of [rednblkx/HomeKey-ESP32](https://github.com/rednblkx/HomeKey-ESP32)
(HomeKit/HomeKey side, HomeSpan) plus:

| Added | Where |
|---|---|
| PN532 over **I2C** (upstream is SPI-only) | `main/Pn532I2cTransport.*`, reader type 3 |
| **Yale/August BLE lock driver** (C++ port of `yalexs-ble`) with state tracking | `main/YaleBleLock.*` |
| **Relay reader**: NFC on a second board, over ESP-NOW | `main/RemoteNfcReader.*`, `main/RelayProtocol.*`, reader type 4 |
| **Relay doorbell** firmware for that second board | `relay-doorbell/` |
| Yale state, door, battery and doorbell entities over MQTT / HomeKit | `main/MqttManager.cpp`, `main/HomeKitLock.cpp`, `main/LockManager.cpp` |

## 1. Architecture

### Two ways to run it

**A. Single device.** One ESP32-C6 runs HomeKit, the NFC reader and the BLE link to
the lock. Validated end to end; the reader holds the HomeKey credentials and the
lock's offline key.

**B. Split, base + doorbell** — the deployed design:

```
   corridor side                          inside the unit
┌────────────────────────┐            ┌──────────────────────────┐
│ doorbell (battery)     │  ESP-NOW   │ base (mains)             │
│  ST25R3916 (or PN532)  │◄──────────►│  HomeKit (WiFi), MQTT    │
│  no keys, no WiFi      │  APDUs     │  HomeKey keys + crypto   │
│  no Bluetooth          │ encrypted  │  BLE ──► Yale nexTouch   │
│  deep sleep            │            │  lock state tracking     │
└────────────────────────┘            └──────────────────────────┘
```

Why: key issuance and revocation happen over HomeKit, so the device holding the keys
must always be reachable. Putting it on mains removes the battery/reachability
tradeoff, keeps credentials off the corridor side and makes revocation immediate.
Estimated ~0.37 Ah/yr for the doorbell versus ~1.5 Ah/yr for a sleeping single
device with hourly check-ins.

Both boards are **Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6**. Doorbell: MikroE NFC 4 Click (ST25R3916),
1S LiPo, button. Base: mains, near enough to the lock for a good BLE signal.

### A tap, end to end

1. The doorbell sleeps; the ST25R3916 sits in wake-up mode. A phone raises the
   antenna amplitude, the chip raises IRQ on D1, the C6 wakes.
2. The doorbell polls with the ECP frame the base gave it, finds the phone and sends
   a `TagEvent` (repeated until the base answers).
3. The base runs the HomeKey transaction, relaying each APDU over ESP-NOW.
4. On success the base sends `ReleaseReq` and asks `YaleBleLock` to unlock; the
   command returns on the lock's ack, the result arrives during the 5 s linger.
5. The doorbell waits for the field to clear, re-arms wake-up mode and sleeps.

### Who knows what (source of truth)

- **Lock and door state: the base.** It is the lock's **only BLE client** (Home
  Assistant's `yalexs_ble` front-door entry is disabled). The base's own BLE status
  read is the authority; the lock's answers to our commands and the Yale cloud state
  forwarded by HA over MQTT are faster inputs that a read confirms.
- **HomeKey credentials and the offline key:** base NVS only. The doorbell holds
  nothing secret except the relay link key.
- **Doorbell health:** the base, from heartbeats (battery, wake statistics), published
  to MQTT.

## 2. Hardware

### ESP32-C6 power figures

Deep sleep 7 µA; BLE RX 71 mA, 802.15.4 RX 74 mA, WiFi RX 78 mA — receive costs are
near-identical, so link choice matters far less than time spent awake. Idle target
for the doorbell: ~20–30 µA (ST25R3916 wake-up mode ~3 µA + C6 deep sleep 7 µA +
regulator).

### Doorbell wiring (production board, XIAO MAC ..AE:A0:98)

Drawing: `docs/wiring.svg`, generated by `docs/wiring.py` — keep it in step with the
`PIN_*` constants in `relay-doorbell/main/main.cpp`.

| XIAO pin | GPIO | Connection | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| D0 / A0 | GPIO0 | battery divider midpoint | `ADC_CHANNEL_0`, `BATTERY_DIVIDER` = 2 |
| D1 | GPIO1 | NFC 4 Click **IRQ** | LP pin: ext1 wake on high, pull-down in sleep |
| D2 | GPIO2 | Click **SDA** | production board, no button (`DOORBELL_BUTTON` 0) |
| D4 | GPIO22 | Click **SCL** | |
| D5 | GPIO23 | — | Click SDA instead when `DOORBELL_BUTTON` is 1 (button on D2 to GND: LP pin, pull-up, ext1 wake on low) |
| 3V3, GND | — | Click power | the Click runs from the XIAO's 3.3 V |
| BAT+ / BAT− pads | — | 1S LiPo (e.g. 503450), JST-PH | check plug polarity |
| — | GPIO3, GPIO14 | on-board RF switch | must be driven, see below |

- **Battery divider:** 1 MΩ + 1 MΩ with 100 nF from the midpoint to GND (~2 µA; 4.2 V
  reads 2.1 V). Not Seeed's suggested 200 kΩ pair (~10 µA — half the idle budget).
  The base ignores readings outside 3000–6000 mV; 0 means not fitted.
- **Stack-up, outside to inside:** doorbell plastic cover → NFC 4 Click, printed
  spiral facing out → **ferrite sheet**, antenna area only, against the Click → XIAO +
  battery behind the chip half. Keep the XIAO's ceramic antenna clear of battery,
  ferrite and metal. The cavity is 36 mm wide.
- **No button on the production board (2026-10-10):** it does not fit the cavity without
  a custom PCB. While re-soldering the Click's joints that morning its SDA wire went to
  D2, so the firmware now puts SDA there (`DOORBELL_BUTTON` 0). The miswire showed as no
  reply on I2C at all; `i2cLineReport()` at boot (idle level of each I2C line with and
  without our pull-up) found it. Without a button there is no "hold the button at
  power-up" way to re-print the relay link key.
- **The 2026-10-09 evening reader fault** (SDA still on D5): the ST25R3916 lost its
  register settings on entering wake-up mode at d_res 2 (calibration read every
  register as 0x00 with the identity intact), antenna readings fell to about half
  (~70–80 at d_res 2 vs ~150) and taps failed, after the doorbell had been mounted and
  handled. Most likely a poor solder joint (power, ground or data): after the joints
  were re-soldered on 10-10 the readings were back to 151 at d_res 2 and calibration
  normal. If it recurs: check the Click's 3V3/GND/SDA/SCL joints first, and consider
  47–100 µF across the Click's 3V3/GND.
- **Old bench doorbell (..8C:DC)** is wired IRQ→D2, button→D1, SDA→D4, SCL→D5 (pins
  swapped 2026-10-08 to match production). It needs the `PIN_*` constants swapped
  back.

### Readers

- **MikroE NFC 4 Click (ST25R3916)** — production, 57.15 × 25.4 mm. Reworked to I2C:
  three 0 Ω `COMM SEL` links moved (ships in SPI), R1 removed to kill the power LED,
  headers replaced by wires (`docs/nfc4-click-rework.pdf`). Identifies as
  `IC_IDENTITY 0x2A (type 0x05 rev 2)` at I2C 0x50. ~3 µA in wake-up mode versus
  milliamps for a PN532 just to look for a card. Rejected alternatives: ELECHOUSE
  board (40.2 mm wide, cavity is 36), NFC 5 Click (ST25R3918, a cut-down 3916 at the
  same price).
- **M5Stack Unit NFC (ST25R3916, bench):** Grove red→XIAO **5V** (USB only),
  black→GND, white = SDA, yellow = SCL (to the doorbell's `PIN_SDA`/`PIN_SCL`). No IRQ
  on the Grove connector. The driver was written against this unit; it polls the
  chip's IRQ registers over I2C, so polling works with **no IRQ wire** — the wire is
  needed only for wake-up mode / deep sleep.
- **PN532 V3 over I2C** (base / single device, bench): VCC→**3V3** (its pull-ups follow
  VCC and the C6 is not 5 V tolerant), SDA→D4 (GPIO22), SCL→D5 (GPIO23) as set on the
  Hardware tab, DIP switches to I2C. 5 V on VCC was tried for a stronger field: no
  change to Express mode, and the rewiring caused reboots. A hung PN532 holds SDA low
  (`clear bus failed`, `I2C scan: no devices`) and **esptool's reset does not
  power-cycle it** — unplug the board's USB.

### XIAO ESP32-C6 RF switch — must be driven

The board routes its antenna through an RF switch: **GPIO3 low enables it, GPIO14 low
selects the built-in ceramic antenna** (high: U.FL). Seeed's Arduino package sets
them; plain IDF and the generic `esp32c6` Arduino variant do not. Floating, it worked
by luck until deep sleep (GPIO3 is an LP pin); then the base heard the doorbell at
−94 dBm instead of −51…−67 and the doorbell could not hear the base at all.

- Doorbell: `selectBuiltInAntenna()` runs first in `app_main`.
- Base: set in `setup()` under `CONFIG_XIAO_ESP32C6_RF_SWITCH` (default y;
  `CONFIG_XIAO_ESP32C6_EXTERNAL_ANTENNA` for U.FL); GPIO3/14 are restricted pins.
- After the fix: base Wi-Fi −75 → **−51 dBm**, relay link → **−64 dBm**. If RSSI ever
  drops ~20–30 dB for no reason, check this first.

## 3. Doorbell firmware (`relay-doorbell/`)

### Reader selection

The doorbell carries both drivers behind the base's `INfcReader`
(`DoorbellPn532Reader.hpp`, and `St25r3916Reader.*` copied verbatim from the base —
keep the two copies identical). Compile default `DOORBELL_DEFAULT_READER` (0 = PN532,
**1 = ST25R3916, default since 2026-10-06**), overridable from NVS `relay/reader`
(u8). On boot it probes the reader's address on a short-lived bus and logs who
answers (`I2C: reader answers at 0x50`, or the whole bus) before the driver claims
the pins. Wake-up mode and deep sleep are ST25R3916-only.

### Polling and tap handling

- The base pushes the ECP frame and timings (`EcpSet`); the doorbell polls with them.
- **ST25R3916 listen window is 25 ms** (`ST25R3916_LISTEN_MS`), not the base's 500 ms.
  500 ms is a PN532 ceiling; the ST25R3916 driver waited it out while the phone was
  not yet answering (~585 ms to find a card). With 25 ms it repeats ECP + WUPA every
  few tens of ms: **111 ms**.
- **One physical tap = one announcement.** After each release the doorbell waits until
  the field has been empty for two polls in a row before announcing again, so a phone
  resting on the reader is one tap however long it stays (a re-detected phone is just
  released). If the field never clears, it gives up after 10 s
  (`FIELD_CLEAR_GIVE_UP_US`).
- `TagEvent` is repeated up to 4× at 250 ms (`TAG_ANNOUNCE_MAX_TRIES`,
  `TAG_ANNOUNCE_RETRY_US`) until an APDU arrives; the card is held until `ReleaseReq`
  or a 2 s fallback. While an announcement is outstanding the loop waits 50 ms, not
  1 s.
- PN532 only: write `CIU_BitFraming` = 0 in `pollOnce()` before the ECP transmit (see
  §8, Express mode).

### Low-power card detection (ST25R3916 wake-up mode)

Build switch `DOORBELL_LPCD` (default 1). The reader rests in wake-up mode
(oscillator off, sampling the antenna every `LPCD_PERIOD_MS` = 100 ms). A phone moves
the amplitude, the chip raises IRQ on D1, and the doorbell polls for `LPCD_ACTIVE_US`
(1.5 s), re-arming once the field is clear. Awake (on USB) the reference is re-taken
every 60 s (`LPCD_RECAL_US`); in deep sleep hourly, on a wake (`LPCD_REARM_SLEEP_US`).

Driver API: `measureAntenna`, `startWakeUpMode(period, ampDelta, phaseDelta, d_res)`,
`takeWakeUpEvents`, `stopWakeUpMode`, `resumeInWakeUpMode`. Sequence and registers
from ST's RFAL (`rfalWakeUpModeStart`, AN5320).

What it takes on the NFC 4 Click:

1. **Weaker drive in wake-up mode.** At full drive the amplitude saturates at 255 (top
   of the A/D) and a phone cannot move it — two minutes of taps, zero wake-ups. Wake-up
   mode uses a reduced TX driver (register `TX_DRIVER` 0x28, `d_res`); polling uses
   full drive. A sweep at first arm picks the strongest drive whose reading is
   ≤ `LPCD_TARGET_MAX` = **160**. Bench Click without ferrite:
   `0:255 1:255 2:217 3:139 4:139 5–14:75 15:42` → d_res 3, ~139. **With the ferrite
   sheet** full drive reads only ~180–187; the old 200 cap chose full drive and it
   false-woke every 1–2 s, so the cap is 160 → d_res 2 (~146). The sweep takes ~6 s,
   runs once per power-up and the result is kept in RTC memory.
2. **Reference from wake-up mode itself.** RFAL's measure command disagreed with the
   mode's own readings (238–251 vs a steady 251), so every arm fired on its first
   sample. Now: arm insensitive (delta 15), let the timer take three readings, adopt
   their average, then tighten the delta without leaving the mode.
3. **Amplitude only.** Phase reads 0 in every mode on this board
   (`LPCD_PHASE_DELTA` = 0; RFAL's default is amplitude-only too).
4. **A phone raises the amplitude** here (the comparison is symmetric): ~+3 at
   detection distance, to ~204 lying on the reader (rest 139); a tap moves it 18–26
   steps. Delta 2 fired on noise; at-rest readings sit within 1–2 steps, so the floor
   is **3** (`LPCD_AMPLITUDE_DELTA`).
5. **Amplitude auto-averaging** (`AMPLITUDE_MEASURE_CONF` 0x33, `am_ae`, weight 16 =
   RFAL's choice): the chip compares against its own running average, which tracks
   slow drift. Without it each false wake re-took the reference right after 1.5 s of
   full-drive polling while the antenna was still "warm", the reading crept 3 steps
   above it, and the next false wake followed — 359 false wakes in 49 min (awake 33 %)
   on a wall charger, 2026-10-08. **Samples that trip the threshold are averaged in
   too** (`am_aam`, RFAL `aaInclMeas`): without it a lasting shift bigger than the
   threshold is never absorbed — mounting the doorbell on a metal plate moved the
   reading 162 → 154 and it woke 16 times in a minute until the adaptive threshold
   reached 9 (2026-10-09). Taps on that metal plate still read at threshold 9 (2/2).
6. **Adaptive sensitivity (under test, 2026-10-09).** The threshold is a share of
   the armed reference, not fixed steps: level L → (`LPCD_PCT_BASE` 2 + L) % of it, at
   least `LPCD_DELTA_FLOOR` 2 steps (3 at a reference of 150 to start; at the top
   level 5, 10 at 150 or 4 at 54). Metal or a laptop can pull the reading from ~150
   to ~50, where a fixed 10 steps would hide a phone (~15 % of the reading).
   `FALSE_WAKES_TO_RAISE` = 3 false wakes within 60 s raise the level; an hour without
   one lowers it. State in `RtcState::lpcdLevel` / `lpcdDelta` (reported in stats).
7. **Drive sweep** with the measure command (~1 ms per d_res; arming wake-up mode per
   step took ~1.4 s each with the settle, and read 0 at d_res 0–2), never stronger
   than `LPCD_DRES_MIN` = 2 (full drive drifts ~8 steps right after a poll). Run at
   power-up and again when the armed reference strays more than `RESWEEP_SHIFT_PCT`
   30 % from the one right after the last sweep (surroundings changed), at most every
   10 min (`RESWEEP_MIN_INTERVAL_US`).
8. **Settle before the reference:** 4 s after a drive sweep, 3 s after a false wake,
   1 s otherwise (`SETTLE_AFTER_SWEEP_MS` / `SETTLE_AFTER_FALSE_WAKE_MS` /
   `SETTLE_NORMAL_MS`, set per arm with `setWakeUpSettleMs`): the driver warms during the
   sweep and during each 1.5 s poll at full drive and the reading drifts for seconds as
   it cools — on battery after power-up (10-10) 160 → 167, 7 false wakes in 29 s, and
   taps in that window failed because the doorbell cannot answer the base while it
   settles. With the longer settles: 0 false wakes in 90 s after power-up. The driver
   waits in wake-up mode (insensitive) before calibrating — right after a poll the reading climbs for
   ~0.6 s (next to a laptop: ref 41 → 50–52, a false wake every 2 s; with the settle,
   1 in 100 s). Wake-up events raised during the settle are cleared; zero readings are
   ignored.

### Deep sleep

Build switch `DOORBELL_SLEEP` (default 1). The C6 sleeps (7 µA) while the ST25R3916
is in wake-up mode and wakes on **D1 high** (phone), **D2 low** (button) or the
**timer** (heartbeat / retry), does that one job and sleeps again.

- **Survives sleep** (`RtcState`, `RTC_DATA_ATTR`, POD only, guarded by `RTC_MAGIC`):
  pinned base MAC and channel, ECP frame, listen/poll timings, `d_res`, the reader's
  wake-up reference (`St25r3916Reader::WakeUpPersist`), adaptive threshold and
  false-wake log, last heartbeat / arm times, wake counters, `baseLost`. Time is
  `gettimeofday` (`rtcNowUs()`), which keeps counting through deep sleep
  (`esp_timer` restarts at 0). A cold boot clears it all. Bump `RTC_MAGIC` when the
  struct changes.
- **Waking from the reader:** `resumeInWakeUpMode()` attaches to the chip *without*
  resetting it (a reset would lose the event), reads the IRQ, then polls.
- **Sleep is refused** while: no base or ECP, reader not in wake-up mode, a tag is
  present or the field not yet clear, the button is held, IRQ is high, a heartbeat is
  unacked, or a frame left < 50 ms ago (`TX_DRAIN_US`).
- **Pins in sleep:** IDF holds LP pad pulls; button pull-up, IRQ pull-down, ext1 per
  pin level (`esp_sleep_enable_ext1_wakeup_io`). After waking, `rtc_gpio_hold_dis` +
  `rtc_gpio_deinit` on both before normal GPIO use.
- **On USB:** the default build *does not sleep while a USB host is attached*
  (`usb_serial_jtag_is_connected()`); it then heartbeats every 30 s (`HEARTBEAT_US`)
  and re-scans after 45 s of silence (`BASE_SILENCE_US`). Every cold boot stays awake
  20 s (`COLD_BOOT_AWAKE_US`) — the window for flashing. Build variants: §6.
- The relay link key is printed only when generated or with the button held at
  power-up — never on every wake.

### Heartbeat and finding the base

- **Heartbeat** every hour (`HEARTBEAT_SLEEP_US`), carrying an ECP request, stats and
  battery. It must be acked (any frame from the base) within 300 ms, 3 tries
  (`HEARTBEAT_ACK_WAIT_US`, `HEARTBEAT_TRIES`). If not: a short search
  (`findBase(2)`), then **re-send the heartbeat** so stats and battery reach the base
  (the `Ping` that finds it carries no battery). If the search fails, keep the pinned
  base and channel and retry in 5 min (`HEARTBEAT_RETRY_US`) — never stay awake
  hunting. The base's silence alarm is 3 h (`HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT_MS`).
- **Tap unanswered** (base restarted on another channel): after 4 unanswered
  announcements (~1 s) it searches once per tap (`researchBase`, up to 30 s, last
  channel first), then deselects the phone and **reads it afresh** (no field-clear
  wait). Re-announcing the old card fails — the phone's session goes stale during the
  search ("Not a HomeKey tag").
- **Button:** the base never replies to a press, so delivery is checked with
  ESP-NOW's send report (`sendConfirmed`); undelivered → search → resend. Presses
  within 1 s are ignored (`BUTTON_REPEAT_US`).
- **No base at power-up** (base down or out of range): on battery the search is
  bounded — 2 sweeps of 13 channels at 250 ms (~7 s, `BASE_SEARCH_ROUNDS`), stretched
  to the 20 s cold-boot window — then `sleepWithoutBase()`, waking on the back-off
  timer (`BASE_SEARCH_BACKOFF_S`: 1, 2, 5, 10, then every 15 min; 30 s in the bench
  build), the button **or a tap**. The reader stays in wake-up mode, so a tap wakes
  the doorbell, it searches (last channel first, usually one ping; for up to 30 s
  after a tap or press, `BASE_SEARCH_ATTENDED_US`, since the base may still be
  starting), and because the reader is *resumed* rather than reset the phone still on
  the reader is served on that same wake once the ECP frame arrives. Without a working
  reader it is powered down. A wake with `RtcState::baseLost` searches like a cold
  boot. On a computer's USB it searches without limit.

### Battery and stats

Battery voltage and stats are **appended to frames the doorbell already sends**
(heartbeats; battery also on tap announcements and button presses), so nothing
transmits solely to report them. The ADC burst is taken on the heartbeat and cached
for tap/button frames (it used to sit between seeing a card and announcing it).

`appendStats()` puts the stats block **ahead of the battery bytes** (the base reads
the battery from the last two bytes, so older firmware on either side still works).
Little-endian:

| Field | Size |
|---|---|
| `'S'`, version (2; base accepts 1 and 2) | 1 + 1 |
| seconds since power-up | u32 |
| awake ms, summed over all wakes | u32 |
| wakes, reader wakes, false wakes, button wakes, timer wakes, cards | u16 each |
| search ms | u32 |
| v2: wake-up threshold, count n | u8 + u8 |
| v2: last n ≤ 4 false wakes, newest first: reading, running average | n × (u8, u8) |

## 4. Base firmware (`main/`)

### Reader types

0 = PN532 SPI (upstream defaults SS=18/MOSI=19/MISO=20/SCK=21), 1 = PN7160,
2 = ST25R3916, **3 = PN532 I2C**, **4 = Relay (ESP-NOW doorbell)**.

PN532 I2C transport: probe 0x24 (three tries — a cold PN532 does not ACK its very
first transaction) and sweep the bus only if that fails (the unconditional 112-probe
sweep cost up to 2.2 s per init). Bus-error logs throttled to one per 5 s per site;
after 20 consecutive bus errors the reader reports disconnected so the 5 s re-init
actually runs.

### Relay reader (`RemoteNfcReader`, type 4)

- **Tap announcements have their own queue** (sharing one with request/response
  replies made the polling wait swallow them). Stale announcements are drained in one
  `pollForTag()` call; no between-poll delay (the queue wait paces it).
- While the BLE link is busy (`g_bleRadioBusy`), announcements are **dropped**, not
  queued; a queued one older than 1.5 s (`TAG_EVENT_MAX_AGE_US`) is discarded.
- During a background status read, an arriving tap sets `g_bleAbort` and that
  announcement is dropped; the doorbell's next repeat (250 ms later) goes through.
- **`releaseTag()` sends `ReleaseReq` even while BLE holds the radio**; only the wait
  for the reply is skipped. A successful tap sets `g_bleRadioBusy` the instant the
  unlock is requested, so a "skip when busy" guard meant no release after any
  successful tap and a 5 s blind window after it.
- Presence checks every 250 ms on the relay (each is a radio round trip), 60 ms
  locally.
- Answers every doorbell heartbeat with a `Pong`; tracks the reader-ready bit carried
  in every doorbell frame; advertises every 3 s when no doorbell is paired; warns if
  a link key is set without a pinned doorbell MAC.
- Logs doorbell stats at most every 10 min and publishes them (`doorbellStats` →
  MQTT). Relay metrics are only emitted for reader type 4.

### Yale BLE driver (`YaleBleLock`)

Connection management:

- **Address type and GATT handles are cached** in NVS (namespace `yaleble`, key
  `gatt`), so a cold start connects directly with no scan or discovery. A failed
  connect does **not** clear the cache — a connection failure says nothing about the
  GATT layout.
- Direct connect to the cached address (`DIRECT_CONNECT_MS` 4 s). After our own
  disconnect the lock is slow to advertise again: use the longer `CONNECT_MS` (10 s),
  never a scan.
- **Scan** (`SCAN_MS` 8 s, `filter_duplicates = 1`) only after three direct connects
  fail in a row (`DIRECT_FAILURES_BEFORE_SCAN`); the counter resets when the scan
  runs, so out of range it scans once per three failures. A failed direct connect
  with a known address means "out of range"; scanning only held the radio to learn the
  same thing. The per-device Yale scan report is parsed and posted at debug level only.
- Connection interval 15–30 ms (`CONN_ITVL_MIN/MAX`), supervision timeout 4 s.
- **Linger 5 s** after a command (`LINGER_MS`, yalexs-ble's default): a second tap
  rides the open session. The lock allows very few concurrent connections and drops
  idle ones after ~30 s; letting go promptly leaves room for the Yale app and Apple
  Home.
- **250 ms cooldown between command writes** (`COOLDOWN_US`), timed from
  command-channel traffic only (`m_lastCmdRxUs`) — timing it from handshake frames
  made every session's first unlock wait 150–220 ms for nothing. Writing too fast can
  crash the lock's radio until its batteries are pulled.
- A repeat of the same lock/unlock request within 2 s is coalesced (`DEDUPE_MS`).
- **Commands return on the lock's ack** (`0xAA`, tens of ms), not its result (`0xBB`,
  ~1.8 s later when the motor stops), so a tap never holds the radio for the
  mechanical cycle; the result arrives during the linger.
- **`g_bleRadioBusy`** is set for the duration of `performCommand()` (connect +
  handshake + ack) and of a status read (`readStatus()`, with
  `g_bleBackgroundRead` so a tap can abort it); it pauses relay traffic. `BusyGuard`
  clears it on return, so the linger does not hold the radio. In range the busy window is ~0.7–1.2 s cold and
  ~0.1 s on a lingering session; out of range ~4 s.
- `waitFor()` returns early on `ConnectFailed`/`DiscDone`, waits in ~50 ms slices
  (so `g_bleAbort` is honoured) and applies cloud reports that land meanwhile. An
  aborted connect does not count as an out-of-range failure.
- **Never log from a NimBLE host callback** — post to the worker task.

### Lock state tracking (since 2026-10-08)

Inputs:

- **Our own commands:** `0xAA` = moving, `0xBB` = done; a non-zero result = jammed
  (as yalexs-ble).
- **Status reads** (GETSTATUS lock `0x02`, door `0x2E`, battery `0x0F`; value at byte
  8, battery bytes 8–9 in mV): at boot +5 s (`BOOT_READ_DELAY_US`), every 5 min
  (`POLL_US`), 10 s after a cloud report that changed the state (`CONFIRM_DELAY_US`),
  15 s after a failed command (`AFTER_FAILURE_READ_US`). Battery only every 6 h
  (`BATTERY_REFRESH_US`). A read takes ~1.7–5.5 s, mostly the connect.
- **Yale cloud state**, forwarded by Home Assistant to `<clientId>/yale/cloud`
  (payloads `locked` / `unlocked` / `jammed` / `door_open` / `door_closed`). The cloud
  sees keypad/thumb-turn/door changes within 1–2 s. Applied at once, then confirmed by
  a read; a report repeating what we already know costs no read.

Rules:

- **The base's read is the authority.**
- **Cloud lock reports are ignored while our own command is in flight** (request → the
  lock's result, max 20 s, `CMD_PENDING_MAX_US`) **and, if they contradict it, for 6 s
  after our result** (`CLOUD_STALE_US`), then a read decides. The cloud runs ~2 s
  behind: a stale "unlocked" from the previous tap once landed mid-"locking" and
  showed for 1.4 s; the other way round it could fake a successful unlock to HA's
  presence automation, which accepts the first "unlocked". Door reports are not
  filtered.
- **Reads never cost a tap.** A read waits while card traffic is under way
  (`RELAY_QUIET_US`, 3 s; retried after `READ_RETRY_US`). If a tap arrives during a
  read, the read stops within ~50 ms and keeps the session, and the doorbell's next
  announcement unlocks on the open session. `request(Unlock|Lock)` pre-empts a read
  the same way.

Outputs:

- `LOCK_OVERRIDE_STATE` → `LockManager`: the HomeKit tile and MQTT lock show the real
  state; moving states set only the target ("Unlocking…"). **The momentary
  snap-back to "locked" is disabled while Yale BLE is enabled**
  (`startMomentaryTimerIfNeeded`).
- `YALE_STATUS` → MQTT and the HomeKit door contact.
- HomeKit / MQTT "lock" sends LOCK (`0x0B`). A failed command puts the tile back to
  the last known state and reads again 15 s later.

### HomeKit accessory

## 5. Protocols

### Relay protocol (`main/RelayProtocol.hpp`, copy in `relay-doorbell/main/`)

ESP-NOW, 10-byte header, fragmented above 240 bytes, one request in flight.
**Unicast is encrypted and both ends are pinned by MAC**; pairing broadcasts are in
the clear because ESP-NOW cannot encrypt broadcast.

**Link key.** The doorbell generates a random 16-byte key on first boot, keeps it in
NVS (`relay/key`) and prints it over USB only when generated (or with the button held
at power-up). It is pasted into the base's web page (Hardware → Link key; masked on
read-back, never logged). Both sides derive PMK = SHA-256(key)[0:16], LMK = [16:32];
the key never crosses the air. The doorbell pins the base's MAC on first contact
(`relay/base`); the base pins the doorbell from its config (`relayDoorbellMac`).
**Both sides add the pinned peer before first contact** — ESP-NOW only decrypts a
unicast from a peer already held with the matching LMK (see §8).

| Frame | Direction | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `Ping` / `Pong` | either | Pairing and channel discovery. The doorbell walks the WiFi channels until a base answers (cached channel `relay/chan` first); an unpaired base advertises every 3 s. The base also answers each heartbeat with a `Pong`. |
| `EcpReq` / `EcpSet` | D→B / B→D | Base hands over the 18-byte ECP frame, the listen window (500 ms) and the gap between cycles (20 ms; 5 with "fast polling"), mirroring `NfcManager::pollingTask()`. |
| `TagEvent` | D→B | A card is present. Repeated up to 4× at 250 ms until an APDU arrives. Has its own queue on the base. |
| `ApduReq` / `ApduRsp` | B→D / D→B | One APDU exchange. |
| `PresentReq` / `PresentRsp` | B→D / D→B | Is the card still there. |
| `ReleaseReq` / `ReleaseRsp` | B→D / D→B | Transaction done; doorbell may move on (2 s fallback if it never comes). |
| `HealthRsp` | D→B | Unsolicited heartbeat (stats + battery); liveness is inferred from traffic, not polled. |
| `ButtonPress` | D→B | Doorbell button (with battery). |

**The base sends nothing between taps** except heartbeat acks: polling was inverted so
the doorbell can sleep and the radio is free for BLE. (The legacy base-driven
`PollReq` path is removed.) Every doorbell frame carries the reader-ready bit.

### Yale BLE protocol (as implemented in `YaleBleLock`)

Service `0xFE24`; every frame is 18 bytes (a 16-byte AES block plus two plain bytes).

- **Secure channel** (`…613` write / `…614` notify), **AES-128-ECB** with the offline
  key: opcode `0x01` exchanges 8 random bytes each way; the session key is
  `ours[0:8] || theirs[0:8]`; opcode `0x03` confirms. Frames carry the key slot at
  `0x11` and a 32-bit checksum at `0x0C`.
- **Command channel** (`…611` write / `…612` notify), **AES-128-CBC**, zero IV, and
  **the chain continues across every frame of the connection** in each direction.
  Frames start `0xEE`; replies `0xAA` (ack) or `0xBB` (result), 8-bit checksum at
  `0x03`; result byte at `0x0F`.
- Opcodes: GETSTATUS `0x02`, UNLOCK `0x0A`, LOCK `0x0B`. GETSTATUS sub-types: lock
  `0x02`, door `0x2E` (door+lock `0x2F`), battery `0x0F`.
- **Both notify characteristics are INDICATE-only** (properties `0x20`). Writing
  `0x0001` to the CCCD is rejected with ATT error `0x80`; write `0x0002`.
- Lock status: `0x00` unknown, `0x01` calibrating, `0x02` unlocking, `0x03` unlocked,
  `0x04` locking, `0x05` locked, `0x06` pol-discovery, `0x07` jammed, `0x0C` secure
  mode. Door: `0x01` closed, `0x02` ajar, `0x03` open.

**Why polling, not push (measured 2026-10-08).** The advertisement is
`020106 030224FE 04FFD10101` + a scan response with the 18-byte Yale ID and name
`M50067E`. The one-byte Yale flag stayed `01` through manual lock/unlock, and there is
no HAP advertisement (Apple `0x004C` type `06`) while the lock's HomeKit side is down
(Apple Home "No Response" since that day, "Accessory not found" on re-add, even after
a battery pull). yalexs-ble's push depends on the HAP GSN or a Yale flag change, so it
gets nothing. `CONFIG_YALE_BLE_LISTEN` (off) keeps a ~5 %-duty passive listener
(50 ms per second) for the GSN should HomeKit come back; with it on, 7 taps showed no
Auth0 errors, but the sample was too small to judge connect latency (median 1.71 s vs
1.33 s baseline).

## 6. Build, flash, configure

```bash
. ~/esp/esp-idf/export.sh                          # ESP-IDF v5.5.5
idf.py set-target esp32c6 && idf.py build          # base firmware
cd relay-doorbell && idf.py build                  # doorbell firmware
```

### Doorbell build variants

| Build dir | Command | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `build` | `idf.py build` | Production: hourly heartbeat, sleeps only when no USB host is attached |
| `build-sleeptest` | `idf.py -B build-sleeptest -DDOORBELL_SLEEP_ON_USB=1 build` | Sleeps on USB: heartbeat 120 s, retry 30 s, base back-off 30 s, ≥ 3 s awake per wake so the port can be caught |
| `build-diag` | `idf.py -B build-diag -DDOORBELL_HEARTBEAT_SLEEP_S=120 build` | Battery diagnostics: heartbeat (and stats) every N s |

Other switches: `DOORBELL_SLEEP`, `DOORBELL_LPCD`, `DOORBELL_DEFAULT_READER`.

Flashing a sleeping doorbell: reset it, wait for the port to appear (20 s cold-boot
window), then flash. The doorbell's app is at 0x10000 (default partition table);
`idf.py flash` leaves its NVS (link key, pinned base) alone — do not erase-flash.

### Base flash layout — preserve NVS

The app outgrew the two-slot OTA layout, so `single_app.csv` gives one 2.875 MB app
partition at `0x20000` and **1 MB for the web UI at `0x300000`**. **OTA is disabled;
flash over USB.** To keep pairing, keys and settings, flash the parts individually,
never the merged image:

```bash
esptool --chip esp32c6 -p PORT write_flash \
  0x0 build/bootloader/bootloader.bin \
  0x8000 build/partition_table/partition-table.bin \
  0x20000 build/HomeKey-ESP32.bin \
  0x300000 build/spiffs.bin        # NVS at 0x9000 is untouched
```

**Back up NVS (`0x9000`, length `0x10000`) before repurposing a board** — reflashing
with different firmware erases the HomeKit pairing, HomeKey keys, WiFi credentials
and the lock's offline key.

**Do not shrink the UI partition** (§8).

### Configuration (base web page or its JSON API)

- Hardware tab: reader type (and I2C pins for types 2/3); relay link key and doorbell
  MAC for type 4.
- HomeKey section: "Always Unlock" (required for tap-to-unlock), Yale BLE toggle, lock
  MAC, key slot, offline key.
- The lock's MAC, slot and offline key come from Home Assistant's `yalexs_ble`
  configuration. **They are deliberately not recorded in this repository.** The
  offline key is masked on read-back and never logged or committed. It lives in NVS,
  which is *not encrypted* — enable flash encryption before deploying.
- MQTT broker and client ID; HA discovery on by default.

## 7. Measurements

### HomeKey over the relay (ESP32-C6 + PN532, 2026-09-15)

| | Direct reader | Relayed over ESP-NOW |
|---|---|---|
| HomeKey transaction | **256 ms** | **338 ms** |
| Endpoint authentication | 174 ms | 187 ms |
| Per-APDU round trip | ~10–20 ms | **42–158 ms (avg 77)** |

Apple's HomeKey tolerates the relay: the iPhone completed transactions with 80–160 ms
per exchange — the risk that could have sunk architecture B. Measured ECP rate
15.2 Hz. With the **ST25R3916** the relayed endpoint authentication is **86–96 ms**,
about half the PN532's.

### Tap to door open (~3.2 s next to the lock, PN532)

| Stage | Time |
|---|---|
| HomeKey exchange (relayed) | 307 ms |
| BLE connect (cached address, no scan or discovery) | 722 ms |
| Secure handshake (30 ms connection interval) | 367 ms |
| **The lock's own unlock and reply** | **1.77 s** |

Over half is the lock itself. Two Express taps 5 s apart (2026-09-16): first
authenticated in 185 ms, connected in 342 ms, unlock done in 2798 ms; second
authenticated in 189 ms and rode the lingering session — **unlock in 437 ms**. Cold ≈
3 s, warm ≈ 0.6 s. (These predate returning on the ack.)

**Range dominates.** Next to the lock: connect 722 ms, unlock 2.9 s. Well away: the
cached-address connect fails, a scan takes 2.6 s, unlock 9.9 s. Weak signal, not
contention, explained the worst measurements — keep the base in good range of the lock
and re-measure before blaming anything else.

### Doorbell (ST25R3916)

- Wake-up mode awake (stage 1, 2026-10-06): 12 min idle, 0 false wakes; 3/3 taps, card
  found 111–112 ms after wake-up, authenticated in 93–94 ms.
- Ferrite production Click at d_res 2: 0 false wakes idle or after taps; 3/3 taps found
  178–234 ms after wake-up.
- From deep sleep: card announced ~336–339 ms after the C6 boots; auth 87–91 ms;
  Express animation confirmed; lock accepted the unlock ~1.5 s later; the doorbell was
  done after 2.1 s (mostly waiting for the phone to leave, ~0.4 s re-taking the
  reference).
- With auto-averaging, deep sleep, bench: 0 false wakes in 12 min (≈90 expected
  before); 3/3 taps found 112 ms after wake-up (average 160, phone 178–186).
- Normal heartbeat wake: **~290 ms**. During a base reboot: 7.9 s, then the retry
  succeeded.
- No base at power-up (base held in bootloader): 20 s search, sleep, 7 s retries,
  found on the first retry after the base came back.
- Base restart on a new channel, before tap-triggered search existed: noticed only at
  the next heartbeat, 52 min later. With it: tap → unlock ~9 s (incl. a slow 4.4 s lock
  connect).

### Yale state tracking (2026-10-08, HA forwarder live)

Manual lock/unlock and door open/close reached the base in the same second the cloud
reported them (cloud 1–2 s behind the lock); the confirming read agreed every time; a
deliberately wrong cloud "unlocked" was corrected by the read 15 s later. Reads took
1.7–5.5 s, mostly the connect (0.6–4.5 s at the base's current spot).

## 8. Hard-won lessons — do not undo

**Radio sharing (base)**
- **Never run BLE and the NFC relay at the same time.** Connecting to the lock
  speculatively when a card was detected corrupted the card exchange ("Auth0 response
  invalid", a 112-byte APDU answered with 0 bytes) and pushed the connect to 15.8 s.
  `g_bleRadioBusy` pauses relay traffic while the lock link is being set up.
- **Do not disable WiFi power save** on the base: saved ~10 ms on the relay but made
  BLE connects 3–4× slower (1.9 s → 4.8–8.0 s). One radio, shared.
- **Do not re-add tap pre-emption** (a tap aborting an in-flight connect,
  `abortLinkAttempt`, 2026-09-16 to 10-06): it never got anyone through the door
  faster, and with the ST25R3916 the same phone, re-detected ~300 ms later, aborted its
  own unlock and fell into `DEDUPE_MS` — one tap, zero unlocks. Taps dropped while the
  link is busy are correct: an unlock is already on its way.
- Do not relax the connection interval during the linger: it would slow the warm tap.
- Never queue tap announcements across a busy window. Symptom of the old behaviour:
  "works on one tap, then nothing for a while, then a burst" (stale taps running 0-byte
  transactions against a phone long gone).

**Relay link**
- **Add the pinned peer before first contact, on both sides.** Learning the peer from
  its first frame deadlocks (the frame cannot be decrypted until the peer is added) —
  happened twice, once per direction. A link key without a pinned MAC cannot pair.
- **Pinning must not skip channel discovery:** the base follows its AP's channel, which
  moved four times in one evening (13 → 6 → 7 → 1).
- **Keep the doorbell's silence timeout longer than its heartbeat interval** (45 s vs
  30 s awake). With both at 30 s the check ran before the ack could arrive and
  re-scanned every 30 s (18 times in 13 min).
- The base must ack heartbeats: without it an idle doorbell "lost" the base every 30 s.
- Anything that must run every loop iteration in the doorbell goes **above** the
  `pollOnce(); continue;` in the main loop (the channel re-scan once sat below it and
  never ran).

**Yale BLE**
- CCCDs need `0x0002` (indicate), not `0x0001`.
- Never log from a NimBLE host callback (it stalled the host task mid-scan).
- Do not clear the GATT cache on a failed connect.
- Never scan after our own disconnect; use the long direct-connect timeout.
- Cloud state is a hint ~2 s behind; never let it override a command in flight.

**NFC**
- **Express mode (PN532):** `CIU_BitFraming` (`0x633D`) must be 0 before every ECP
  transmit. Anticollision sends REQA/WUPA as 7-bit short frames and can leave
  `TxLastBits = 7`; the next `InCommunicateThru` sends the ECP frame's last byte as 7
  bits, the iPhone fails the CRC and ignores it. Symptom: taps only work with the Home
  Key opened in Wallet, while the frame looks perfect from the host. The base's old
  `Pn532Reader::healthCheck()` did this write "as a liveness probe"; it broke when
  polling was inverted. The doorbell does it in `pollOnce()`.
- ST25R3916 wake-up mode: see the six points in §3 (drive cap 160 with ferrite,
  reference from the mode itself, amplitude only, delta floor 3, auto-averaging,
  adaptive threshold). Listen window 25 ms, not 500.

**Hardware / tooling**
- Drive the XIAO RF switch (§2).
- PN532: 3V3 only; a hung one needs a USB unplug.
- **Do not shrink the UI partition.** The UI is ~120 KB; with a 128 KB partition
  LittleFS silently truncated every file (`components.js` served 9,956 of 45,799 bytes)
  and the page still rendered from browser cache — new fields never appeared. If a UI
  change "doesn't show up", compare served sizes against `data/dist/assets/*.gz`.
- The last log line before deep sleep needs `fflush` + `fsync` + 200 ms or it is lost.
- The doorbell's USB console goes quiet after a reset until the port re-enumerates;
  reopen the port rather than assuming a crash. Unplugging USB kills any attached
  serial capture.

**Logging and efficiency (2026-09-16 pass)**
- Keep hot-path logs at **debug**: per-APDU round trip, relay summary, ECP hex on push,
  "doorbell asked for ECP", the doorbell's poll-cycle stats. 8–12 INFO lines per tap
  once starved the WebSocket during the timing-critical transaction.
- Doorbell: reassembly buffer moved, not copied; no 260-byte zero-init per frame in the
  receive callback. Base: `Response` objects drained before their queues are deleted.
- Rejected on purpose: the PN532 transport's fixed 301-byte reads (~7 ms per response;
  that reader is being replaced and the fix needs PN532 re-serve semantics verified on
  hardware).

## 9. Known issues / next steps

1. **Doorbell false wake-ups on battery — adaptive threshold under test.**
   Auto-averaging fixed the wake-up loop on charger power (0–1 false wakes overnight),
   but **on battery 7172 false wakes in 6 h** were seen on 2026-10-09. The adaptive
   threshold and stats v2 (§3) are being tested now; watch `doorbell/stats`
   (`threshold`, `false_log`, `awake_pct`), ideally with the `build-diag` variant.
   **Most likely cause (not proven — that firmware recorded no readings):** a lasting
   shift in the antenna reading when it was unplugged/moved at 07:33, which a fixed
   threshold of 3 with triggering samples excluded from the average could never absorb
   (re-arming does not reset the chip's running average). Mounting it on a metal plate
   that afternoon reproduced the mechanism (162 → 154, 16 wakes until the adaptive
   threshold reached 9); `am_aam` lets one wake absorb such a shift. To confirm:
   unplug/move it a few times on the `am_aam` build — at most one false wake each. A
   healthy doorbell shows an awake share well under 0.1 %. Background: the battery fell
   3982 → 3540 mV in 9 h of testing on 2026-10-08, far more than taps and searches
   explain (~20 mAh).
2. **Measure real consumption** by battery voltage over days plus a multimeter in series
   (no PPK2).
3. **Doorbell button on the final hardware:** verified on the bench (2026-10-08 — presses
   reached the base, including a wake from deep sleep on battery); still to wire to the
   SadoTech switch.
4. **The lock's HomeKit side is down** (since 2026-10-08), so no state is broadcast;
   the base polls. If HomeKit comes back, consider `CONFIG_YALE_BLE_LISTEN`.
5. **Own key slot for the base.** The base shares the offline key and slot with HA's
   (now disabled) `yalexs_ble` config: safe per session (fresh session keys per
   connection), but a key rotation breaks both, and unlocks cannot be attributed. Fix:
   create a dedicated account, invite it to the lock, open the lock with it once over
   Bluetooth in person so the key is *loaded*, then extract key and slot.
6. **AP channel changes.** The base follows its AP; a sleeping doorbell finds out when a
   tap or press goes unanswered and searches then, so a tap after a channel change
   costs seconds rather than failing.
7. **Relay round trips run 28–160 ms**, above ESP-NOW's usual 5–15 ms; mostly the
   iPhone's own crypto. The relay adds ~40 ms to a whole transaction: low priority.
8. **Diagnostics still compiled in:** I2C bus scan, BLE scan reports, relay statistics,
   the doorbell's poll-cycle rate (every 1000 cycles) and the ECP frame on push.
9. **Security before leaving it running:** web UI authentication is off by default;
   enable it. Enable flash encryption — the offline key and relay link key sit in plain
   NVS.
